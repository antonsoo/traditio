Scope: selected clauses of Institutes 2.77-79. No later Digest or Justinian
compromise is imported into this lesson. Page 162 retains writing, painting
and the opening examples; page 163 retains the rival views and remedies.
The lower part of page 163 begins an earlier-numbered section in this edition;
it is surrounding editorial context, not a second part of 2.79.

Transcription joins line wraps, prints consonantal u as v, retains spelling
such as inpensam, conpetit and subripuerit, marks supplied words with angle
brackets and omissions with [...], displayed as a bracketed ellipsis. It
does not silently import later additions or a rule of reversibility.

Our close English and phrase notes are new, not a quotation of Poste's
translation or an independently reviewed philological edition. In particular,
tabula is rendered board/panel, not the historical translation's canvas.

The schools in 2.79 offer rival answers, not shared ownership. Gaius names
Sabinus and Cassius but calls their rivals the opposing school here. We do
not claim a philosophical affiliation for either school. The writing and
painting paragraphs separately report their rules and qualify recovery.
Theft is an added premise in the remedy example; making does not prove theft.
The repair example is an undecided modern analogy. No complete survey of
Roman law or present-day legal advice is offered.
