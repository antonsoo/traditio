translation and commentary by Edward Poste (1823-1902). Fourth edition,
revised and enlarged by E. A. Whittuck (1844-1924), with an introduction
by A. H. J. Greenidge (1865-1906). Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1904.

Downloaded as JPEGs from the item's page-image endpoint on 2026-10-29.
Printed page numbers were checked against the visible images. The endpoint
uses n=(printed page + 63) for these pages; this is not the leafNum field
in the item's OCR page-number metadata. The images have not been cropped,
retouched, generated or relabelled as an ancient manuscript.

The 1904 text and faithful page reproductions are public-domain material.
The project's PolyForm Noncommercial license covers its own code, drawings, new translations
and word notes; it does not replace third-party font licenses.

Transcription policy: join printed line wraps; print consonantal u as v;
retain spelling such as conpletur, inpleta, conpetit and adfectu; use angle
brackets for editorially supplied words and [...] for omitted text. The
project's readable English is a new close translation, distinct from the
1904 English visible on each page. Textual restorations are evidence of an
editor's reading, not silent claims about a complete ancient witness.

Roman law treated enslaved people as property and distributed legal
capacity unequally. That evidence and historical translations remain in
the complete pages. The workshop's invented cases use objects and land.
