# Traditio design context

An English-speaking learner should discover that physical custody, civil title,
and Gaius's in bonis can travel on different tracks. The task is to change one
fact, observe an outcome, and interrogate the Latin condition that caused it.
This is a bounded reading of a second-century jurist, not a general legal engine.

## Direction

An illustrated legal case sheet, with a large diagram of the transfer and three
parallel tracks. The transfer, not a marketing hero, dominates the first screen.
There are no statue photographs, invented archaeological artifacts, gradients,
dashboard cards, decorative numerals, or simulated manuscript textures.

Palette: paper #fcfdff, board #edf2f6, ink #172b43, possession blue #286491,
civil-title violet #705181, in-bonis green #276457, annotation brass #80601c.
Type: Literata for the central question and Latin; Source Sans 3 for facts,
controls and explanation. Left alignment; text columns under 75 characters.

Desktop layout:

    name                                  Reading / Scope / Keep a case
    Who owns the mule?            Gaius, second century CE
    [Delivery] [Good faith] [Land]
    facts to change    |  Lucius ---- object ---- Aulus
                      |  possession ----------- Aulus
                      |  civil title --- Lucius
                      |  in bonis ------------- Aulus
    year 0 ---- year 1 ---- year 2 ---- year 3
    explanation        |  Latin condition + close English
    pinned case        |  current case (exact comparison table)
    source reader and full scanned pages

Phone: facts, diagram, timeline, result, Latin. The diagram uses a smaller
coordinate layout, never shrunken desktop text. Controls keep focus when the
model redraws. Meaning is encoded in text and position as well as color.

The initial cream-and-red antiquarian direction was rejected: it repeated the
existing collection. The blue field and three ownership tracks come from this
lesson's distinction and remain useful without historical ornament.

## Counterexample exercise

A second task follows the comparison: choose an intentionally overbroad claim,
submit the current case as a fixed witness, and collect passages from the existing
Latin reader. The learner should have to distinguish a false conclusion from a
failed premise. Provincial land stays a boundary, never a fabricated counterexample.

Use one ruled argument sheet, with the claim and submission on the left and the
citations and learner's explanation on the right. No points, badges, answer cards,
or auto-graded prose. A checked case is a snapshot: changing the live controls must
not quietly change the argument. Show its full facts and an explicit restore action.
On phones the claim, witness, citations, and explanation follow in that order.
The portable casebook must carry the same argument, its facts, and source links.
